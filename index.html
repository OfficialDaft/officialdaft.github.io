Gabe Newell
Born: November 3, 1962
Title: Chief Executive Officer
Affiliation: Valve Corporation
Politics: Hato – Reformists
Nickname(s): GabeN, Lord Gaben, The Valve

Gabe Logan Newell (born November 3, 1962), nicknamed GabeN or Gaben, is the co-founder and president of Valve, the studio behind Half-Life, Counter-Strike, Portal, Dota 2 and the Steam platform. In SAKA: Cyberpunk he appears as a meme-flavored Chief Executive Officer who renames the corporation to Valve Corporation and wanders the Tower talking to everyone.

This entry is split in two. Part I covers the real person and company using public information. Part II covers the SAKA character. They are kept apart on purpose.

PART I

Real Life

The public record on Gabe Newell and Valve: history, business, memes, interviews and documentaries.

Early life

Newell was born in Aspen, Colorado, and grew up in Davis, California, where he attended Davis Senior High School. He taught himself programming and has said he expected to become a doctor. He enrolled at Harvard in 1980 to study programming.

Microsoft (1983–1996)

While at Harvard he visited his brother at Microsoft and dropped out to join. He spent about 13 years there and is credited with producing the first three releases of Windows. In the mid-1990s he led the port of Doom to Windows 95, helping make Windows a credible gaming platform. The success of Doom distribution helped inspire his view that software distribution was about to change—a thread that leads to Steam.

Founding Valve (1996)

On August 24, 1996, Newell and fellow Microsoft employee Mike Harrington left to found Valve in Washington state, inspired in part by Michael Abrash’s move to id to work on Quake. Newell married Lisa Mennet the same day. Harrington sold Microsoft shares to fund his half; Newell borrowed against his shares for his.

Half-Life and the near-bankruptcy (1997–1998)

Valve licensed the Quake engine and built Half-Life on a heavily modified version. After an outstanding E3 showing, the studio was burning through money. The game as it stood wasn’t good enough, so the team scrapped large parts of it and restarted. Newell took a loan against Valve’s future to keep production alive. Half-Life released in November 1998, won more than 50 Game of the Year awards, and sold about 2.5 million copies in its first twelve months.

Sierra, Counter-Strike and independence (1998–2003)

Sierra owned the Half-Life IP and had rights to Valve’s next games. Valve eventually won back control of its IP and future. The company hired the creators of the Counter-Strike mod, which became one of the most-played online games ever. In 2000 Harrington sold his stake to Newell, leaving him the majority owner.

Half-Life 2, Steam and the Vivendi suit (2002–2004)

Newell gave the Half-Life 2 team no deadline and a nearly unlimited budget. Meanwhile, he spent months on Steam, announced in 2002 and launched in September 2003. Players initially resented it, as it was required to update games like Counter-Strike. A hacker stole Half-Life 2 source code in September 2003; the game finally shipped in November 2004. Valve’s legal fight with Vivendi, Sierra’s parent, nearly bankrupted both the company and Newell personally.

The golden era (2007–2013)

2007The Orange Box ships with Team Fortress 2, Portal and Half-Life 2: Episode Two, which ends on a cliffhanger.
2008–2009Left 4 Dead arrives, followed by a sequel the next year.
2011Portal 2 launches, and the first Dota 2 International offers a then-huge prize pool.
2012–2013Counter-Strike: Global Offensive launches; Dota 2 leaves beta. Valve announces SteamOS and the Steam Machine concept.

Hardware and VR (2013–2022)

The Steam Machines launched in 2015 with Valve’s Steam Controller and Steam Link, and sold poorly. Valve partnered with HTC on the Vive, launched its own Valve Index, and shipped Half-Life: Alyx in March 2020. The Steam Deck was revealed July 15, 2021 and shipped in February 2022.

2023–2026

Counter-Strike 2 replaced CS:GO in September 2023, a Steam Deck OLED followed that November, and Valve released a long Half-Life 25th anniversary documentary. On November 12, 2025 Valve announced the Steam Machine, a new Steam Controller and the Steam Frame VR headset. Fans nicknamed the cube-shaped Machine the “GabeCube.”

Philosophy and public statements

  • Piracy: in 2011 he argued piracy is a service problem rather than a price problem.
  • Consoles and rivals: he criticized PS3 development, Xbox Live and Windows 8 in public remarks.
  • Work culture: Valve is famous for its flat structure, desks on wheels and a new-hire handbook that leaked publicly.
  • Open hardware: he wanted Steam Deck owners to install anything they like, including non-Steam software.

Why can’t Gabe count to three?

The joke is that Valve never releases a “3.” Half-Life 2 ended with Episode Two, and Portal 2, Left 4 Dead 2, Team Fortress 2, Counter-Strike 2 and Dota 2 all stop at two. Theories include Valve’s perfectionism, impossible expectations, the success of Steam, its flat structure and cancelled projects. Newell has also played along with the meme in interviews and game appearances.

Beyond Valve

  • Starfish Neuroscience: neural-interface research co-founded with Philip Sabes.
  • Inkfish: a marine research organization with ships and submersibles.
  • Oceanco: the yacht builder he acquired in August 2025.
  • foundry10: an education research group he co-founded.
  • Heart of Racing: a racing team supporting children’s charities.
  • Charity: a Gnome Chompski figure was launched into space with a donation benefiting Auckland’s Starship Children’s Hospital.

Personal life and wealth

Newell married Lisa Mennet on Valve’s founding day, and they have two sons. Newell has spoken about having Fuchs’ dystrophy, a corneal disease, and receiving transplants. Forbes has reported estimates of his fortune that have changed substantially over the years; such figures are estimates and may change.

Honors

IGN ranked him among top game creators in 2009, and in 2013 he received the BAFTA Fellowship and a place in the AIAS Hall of Fame.

Interviews and documentaries

  • Geoff Keighley, “The Final Hours of Half-Life 2” (2004) and later Final Hours coverage of Alyx.
  • LinuxCon keynote (2013) on open source and gaming; Reddit AMA (2014).
  • Half-Life 25th anniversary documentary (Valve, 2023) and Half-Life 2 20th anniversary documentary (2024).
  • Interviews with Forbes, Washington Post, IGN, TVNZ and other outlets.

Controversies and criticism

  • Steam’s 30% revenue cut and platform dominance have drawn antitrust criticism and lawsuits.
  • Skin-gambling and loot-box markets around Valve games drew regulatory and legal attention.
  • Valve’s opaque communication and slow output are perennial complaints, sometimes called Valve Time.

Gaben in pop culture

Fans turned “Gaben” into “Lord Gaben,” and Newell has said he is learning to roll with the hugs. Steam sales are jokingly attributed to him. To fans he is part folk hero, part running joke, and to critics the face of a powerful storefront.

PART II

SAKA: Cyberpunk

The Roblox character: Valve Corporation’s CEO, meme, and friendly face of the Tower.

Author’s note

Written by officialdaft on Discord, stickemup on ROBLOX. This interpretation of Gabe Newell is supposed to be a meme within SAKA but also to let others enjoy having someone who walks around and talks to everyone. Whenever he is appointed CEO, the first thing he will do is rename anything SAKA-related to Valve and implement Steam within the Corporation. The lore follows the real-life perspective with some portions shifted to fit the SAKA universe.

Appointment protocol

Whenever GabeN is appointed CEO in any SAKA server, he renames the corporation to Valve Corporation before anything else. Signage, badges, terminals and other SAKA-branded items are relabeled. Next comes the rollout of Steam across the Corporation. Staff still say “SAKA” out of habit, and Traditionalists call the rename a vanity project.

Server behavior

1. Appointed CEO → 2. Rename everything SAKA to Valve → 3. Implement Steam in the Corporation → 4. Start walking around and talking to people.

SAKA biography

The in-universe biography follows the real one with the serial numbers filed off.

Project Half-LifeThe division’s first project nearly collapsed in the lower floors. Scrapped, restarted and finished on borrowed money, it earned the division its seat on the Executive floor.

AscensionAppointed CEO, he renamed the megacorporation Valve Corporation.

Steam in the Corporation

His signature policy is rolling Steam out as the internal platform for the whole Corporation. In the fiction, Steam is the Tower’s distribution and software network.

  • Employees receive tools, programs and updates through a single store.
  • Departments publish internal mods and equipment configurations through a Workshop-style system.
  • Seasonal sales apply to company supplies, which staff find both delightful and suspicious.
  • Staff are encouraged to carry portable terminals, nicknamed Decks.

Walks, talks and meetings

Newell is the Tower’s friendliest executive. He walks the floors and chats with anyone, even while meetings are in progress. Agendas slip, minutes go missing, and nobody is quite willing to interrupt the CEO. That slow pace is the SAKA version of Valve Time.

Count to three, SAKA edition

Under Valve Corporation, no initiative ever reaches a third phase. Projects have a Phase One and a Phase Two, then fall quiet. Ask when Phase Three starts and he will tell you, deadpan, that the number must not be said.

Politics

Newell belongs to the Hato – Reformists. He wants a better life for employees and a corporate culture that lasts, which in practice means better tools, humane schedules and open systems.
